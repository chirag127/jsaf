Freelancing in AI: earning $100 to $250 an hour from India

Freelance AI work is the fastest way to raise your income without leaving your day job. The rates are genuinely high because the skills are scarce and the work is delivered entirely across the internet. The catch is that you are not hired at those rates from day one. You earn them by building case studies, not by posting a profile. Here is the full picture, from entry rates to value-based pricing to the legal setup that keeps the income clean.

Senior AI consulting runs 130 to 220 percent above traditional tech freelance rates. That premium exists because AI work is new, risky for the client, and hard to verify. The client pays for someone who has shipped it before and can be held accountable. That is the position you are moving into, and it is why the ceiling is so high.

Project sizes and India's entry floor

Startup AI projects run 5,000 to 25,000 dollars. Enterprise projects run 15,000 to 60,000 dollars. For India-sourced work, the project floor is around 3,500 dollars, which works out to roughly 60 to 110 dollars an hour in equivalent value. North American projects start at 10,000 dollars and above, equivalent to 175 to 250 dollars an hour. The gap between those two numbers is where your domain expertise lives. A generic prompt engineer does the floor. A person who understands ERP, CPQ, and supply chain support automation does the North American band.

Your pricing model, in three stages Stage one is hourly, while you are proving yourself. Stage two is project-based, 5,000 to 80,000 dollars, once you understand the shape of the work. Stage three is value-based, 10 to 15 percent of the documented client ROI. A retainer of 3,000 to 15,000 dollars a month for AI advisory is the stable middle. Move up the ladder as your case studies accumulate. Do not jump straight to value-based pricing. You need the ROI documentation first.

Realistic monthly income, stated plainly

Once you have two or three case studies, 10 to 20 hours a week of consulting earns roughly 150,000 to 400,000 rupees a month in India. The top freelancers doing specialist work clear 150 to 250 dollars an hour. Those are not fantasy numbers. They are the public bands for the work. The difference between you and them is shipped evidence, not talent.

The niche that fits you, specifically

Do not compete in the generic AI marketplace. You have a domain moat that a bootcamp graduate does not. Your niche is LLM-powered quote and configuration automation for SAP and ERP clients. Pricing anomaly detection. Procurement copilots. Support automation for CPQ and S/4HANA workflows. This is a consultable niche in the 150 to 250 dollar global band, and it is scarce and underserved. A public case study of an LLM-assisted quote or configuration flow is the single asset that opens that niche.

The setup that keeps it clean

Run the freelance income through the structure in Article 7 and Article 8. Income is business or profession income. File ITR-3 or ITR-4. Pay advance tax. Register for GST at 20 lakh of turnover, and file the LUT for zero-rated exports. Receive foreign payments through SWIFT, PayPal, Payoneer, or Wise, with a FIRC or FIRA, and repatriate within nine months. Use purpose code P0802 or P0806. If you stay under the thresholds and use the presumptive route, remember the 2026-27 rules live under Section 58, not the old 44AD.

The one rule that protects your day job Freelance work must be completely separate from TCS. Own equipment, off-hours, no TCS confidential data, no TCS clients, no conflict with TCS competitors. The moment the two overlap, you are not earning freelance income. You are committing a breach of contract with potential criminal exposure. Keep a written log of the separation from day one.

Where to find the work

The marketplace floor is on the bigger platforms, but the premium work comes through referral and a public portfolio. Your three projects from Article 6, posted on GitHub with clean READMEs and a one-page personal site, are the marketing. Follow the LLM application practitioners on X and LinkedIn. They post the work that pays. The niche SAP and ERP AI consulting is small enough that one strong case study and one visible blog post will get you inbound leads. You are not competing with ten thousand generic prompt engineers. You are the only person in the room who has shipped an ERP-aware LLM system.

The close

Freelancing is a side engine, not a full-time exit, until you have three case studies and two paying clients. Then it becomes a real income line, and possibly the reason you negotiate your next salary from a position of strength. Start with the internal case study. Make it public. Let the domain moat do the selling. The rates in this article are real, but they are earned by showing the work, not by asking for it.
