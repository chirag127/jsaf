The 14 to 18 month learning roadmap

This is the machine. It assumes you work 9.5 hours a day at TCS and can carve out 8 to 10 hours a week for learning. Two evenings of two hours, one weekend block of four hours. That is about 40 hours a month, which is one portfolio-quality project every three months. The entire hard cost of this plan is about 150 to 260 dollars. Everything else is free.

The skill you actually need, and the skill you do not

You do not need to know everything about AI. The 2026 applied market rewards a narrow, verifiable stack. Solid intermediate Python, then classical machine learning intuition, then large language model application engineering, then one cloud deployment, then one deep domain project. The stack in order, not the textbook in order.

Skip, with confidence Deep linear algebra proofs, classical computer vision research, robotics, and reinforcement learning theory. A light pass is fine. These do not show up in the applied interview loops we are targeting. Your hours are better spent shipping one RAG system than proving a gradient identity.

The cheapest entry credential The AWS Certified AI Practitioner is 100 dollars, 90 minutes, 65 questions, valid for three years. It is the single cheapest way to put a cloud AI credential on your resume. AWS's own recommended next step is the ML Engineer-Associate, but you do not need that one to get hired. One cloud credential plus a shipped project is the 2026 bar.

What you should know at each gate

Gate one, after two months. You write Python without a tutorial open. You understand a training split, bias-variance, and when a model overfits. Gate two, after five months. You can explain why attention replaced RNNs, what an embedding is, and the difference between a generative and a discriminative model. Gate three, after ten months. You have a RAG pipeline you built, you have evaluated it on a golden set of about 50 questions, and you can explain why you chose your chunk size. Gate four, after fourteen months. Your three projects are on GitHub with clean READMEs, and your resume has been rewritten in the new vocabulary.

The habit that halves the project cost

Build the portfolio from your own work artifacts. Every ticket pattern you see at TCS becomes a dataset sketch. Every customer question you answer becomes an evaluation pair. Keep a golden-qa file open on your machine. Add one real CPQ question and your answer to it, every week. In twelve months, you have a private evaluation set that no course gives you. That is the single highest-leverage habit in this entire book.

The math of the money

The total hard cost is roughly 100 dollars for the AWS exam, plus 50 to 160 dollars if you pay for one Coursera certificate and one more exam. That is the 150 to 260 dollar range. Course audits are free. fast.ai is free. The SAP tutorials are free. The Kaggle micro-courses are free. You are not paying for knowledge in this plan. You are paying for a credential and a small margin of convenience. If money is tight, skip the paid certificate. The GitHub ships the message, not the badge.
